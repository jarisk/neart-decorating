How long does it take to paint a room?

Updated 21 November 2026By Neart Decorating, Cardiff6 min read
Quick answer

For a professional decorator, an average bedroom takes one to two days for walls, ceiling and woodwork with two coats. A small room with good walls can be done in a day. A living room usually takes two to three days, and a hallway, stairs and landing two to four days. If you’re doing it yourself, allow roughly twice as long.

How long does it take to paint each room?

These are typical times for an experienced decorator painting walls and ceilings in two coats, plus the woodwork (skirting, frames and doors), in a room that needs normal preparation:

RoomProfessionalTypical DIY
Box room or small bedroom1 day1–2 days
Double bedroom1–2 days2–3 days
Living room2–3 days3–5 days
Hallway, stairs and landing2–4 days4–7 days
Kitchen (walls and ceiling)1–2 days2–3 days
Bathroom1 day1–2 days

Walls only, with no ceiling or woodwork, roughly halves the time. Heavy filling, a dark-to-light colour change or very high ceilings add to it.

Hallways and stairwells take longer than their size suggests. There are lots of doors, frames and spindles to paint, and the high stairwell wall needs a safe platform to reach. For prices to go with these timings, see our painter and decorator prices guide.

How long does it take to paint a 12x12 room?

A 12 ft × 12 ft room (about 3.7 m × 3.7 m) is a typical double bedroom. For a professional, the full job of walls, ceiling and woodwork usually takes one to two days:

  • Preparation: moving and covering furniture, filling, sanding and masking, about 2–3 hours
  • Ceiling: two coats, about 2–3 hours plus drying time
  • Walls: cutting in and rolling two coats, about 3–4 hours plus drying time
  • Woodwork: skirting, frames and door, about 3–4 hours spread across the job

Just the walls in a 12x12 room with no repairs is often half a day to a day. In an older Cardiff terrace with ceilings around 2.7–3 m, add a little more time for the extra wall area and the ladder work.

Can painters do two coats in one day?

Yes, with water-based emulsion in most conditions. Modern emulsions are usually dry enough to recoat after a few hours, so a decorator can put the first coat on in the morning and the second in the afternoon. It is one reason a professional can finish a room so much faster.

It depends on the conditions, though. In a cold, damp or poorly ventilated room, paint dries much more slowly, and rushing the second coat leads to drag marks and patchiness. Always go by the recoat time on the tin rather than how the wall feels.

Solvent-based (oil) gloss and undercoat are different. They usually need overnight or longer before the next coat, which is why woodwork often runs across two days.

How long does paint take to dry between coats?

PaintTouch dryReady to recoat
Water-based emulsion (walls and ceilings)1–2 hoursUsually 2–4 hours
Water-based satin or eggshell (woodwork)1–2 hoursUsually 4–6 hours
Solvent-based gloss or undercoat4–6 hoursUsually 16–24 hours
Mist coat on new plaster1–2 hoursUsually a few hours, often left overnight

Typical figures. Always check the tin, as drying times vary between products, temperature and humidity.

“Dry” is not the same as “hard”. Emulsion keeps curing for two to four weeks, so be gentle with fresh walls for the first fortnight. Painting new plaster? It needs a mist coat first, and the plaster must be fully dry before that.

What makes painting a room take longer?

  • Preparation: cracks, holes, flaking paint and patched plaster all need filling and sanding before any paint goes on.
  • Colour changes: covering a dark or strong colour often needs an extra coat. See painting over old paint.
  • Woodwork: doors, frames, spindles and windows are slow, careful work.
  • Furniture: a full room takes longer to clear, protect and work around than an empty one.
  • Wallpaper: stripping old paper and repairing the wall underneath can add a day on its own.
  • Conditions: cold, damp rooms slow down drying between coats.

How long does it take to paint a room yourself?

Most people underestimate the preparation and the cutting in. As a rule of thumb, allow about twice the professional time: a full weekend for a bedroom, and longer if you are doing the ceiling and woodwork too. Plan for drying time between coats, and don’t forget the clean-up and putting the room back together at the end.

If time matters more than cost, for example before moving in or between tenants, a decorator who can put two coats on in a day and work around drying times will have the room back in use far sooner. See our interior painting service.

Frequently asked questions

How long should paint dry before sleeping in a room?+

With modern water-based emulsion and good ventilation, most people are comfortable sleeping in a room the same night once the windows have been open for a few hours. Solvent-based gloss on woodwork smells for longer, so allow a day or two with the windows open, and longer for anyone sensitive to fumes.

How long before you can hang pictures after painting?+

Emulsion is dry to the touch in a few hours but keeps hardening for a couple of weeks. Wait at least a few days before hanging pictures or pushing furniture back against the walls, and avoid scrubbing the walls for two to four weeks.

Does a second coat take as long as the first?+

Usually less. The first coat includes cutting in carefully around every edge, while the second coat goes on faster over a surface that is already sealed and even.

How long does it take to paint a whole house?+

For a typical three-bedroom house, a full interior repaint of walls, ceilings and woodwork usually takes a small team around one to two weeks, depending on the condition of the walls and how much woodwork there is.
