Can you paint over wallpaper, gloss or old paint?

Updated 21 November 2026By Neart Decorating, Cardiff7 min read
Quick answer

Old paint: yes, if it is sound. Clean, fill and lightly sand first. Gloss: yes, but sand it to a dull finish and use a primer or undercoat, because emulsion won’t stick to it directly. Wallpaper: you can, but it often causes bubbles and visible seams, so stripping and lining or making good the wall is usually the better long-term option.

Can you paint over wallpaper?

Sometimes, but it is rarely the best option. Painting over wallpaper can work if the paper is:

  • firmly stuck down everywhere, with no lifting seams, corners or bubbles
  • plain or lightly textured, such as woodchip or lining paper, rather than vinyl or heavily patterned
  • clean and free of grease

The problem is moisture. Water-based paint can soften the paste, so the paper bubbles or the seams lift, sometimes weeks later. Seams and pattern can show through the new colour, and vinyl wallpaper has a plastic surface that emulsion does not grip well. Once paper has been painted, it is also much harder to strip in future.

If you do paint over wallpaper

  1. Stick down any loose edges with border adhesive and let it dry.
  2. Wipe the walls down and let them dry fully.
  3. Apply a solvent-based or stain-blocking primer. This seals the paper so the emulsion’s moisture can’t soak into the paste.
  4. Finish with two coats of emulsion.

Test a small, hidden area first. If it bubbles, stop: the paper needs to come off.

The better long-term option

Strip the paper, repair the wall, and either paint directly or hang lining paper for a smooth paintable surface over uneven walls. It takes longer up front, but the finish is far better and lasts. Our wallpapering team strips, lines and hangs paper, and can decorate the whole room in one go.

Can you paint over gloss?

Yes, but not straight on. Gloss is smooth and hard, so new paint needs a key to grip:

  1. Clean with sugar soap to remove grease and grime, especially on doors and handrails.
  2. Sand the whole surface with fine abrasive until the shine is gone, then remove the dust.
  3. Prime or undercoat. When going from oil-based gloss to a water-based satin or eggshell, use a primer or undercoat made for the job.
  4. Apply your top coats in thin, even layers.

Not sure what’s on your woodwork? Rub a small area with a cloth dampened with methylated spirits. If paint comes off onto the cloth, it is water-based. If nothing comes off, it is probably oil-based gloss and needs proper sanding and priming before a water-based paint goes on.

Old oil-based gloss on skirting and doors often turns yellow, especially in rooms without much daylight. Water-based woodwork paints stay whiter for longer, which is why many people switch when they redecorate.

Can you paint over old paint?

Yes. Most redecorating is painting over existing paint. The key is that the old paint must be sound:

  • Wash walls in kitchens, bathrooms and hallways to remove grease and fingerprints.
  • Scrape off anything flaking or peeling back to a firm edge, then feather the edges with sandpaper.
  • Fill cracks and holes, let the filler dry and sand it flush.
  • Spot-prime bare plaster and filler, otherwise they show through as dull patches.
  • Lightly sand silk or sheen finishes so the new coat grips.

If paint is peeling in large areas, find out why first. Damp, condensation or an incompatible layer underneath will make the new paint fail in the same way.

Old Cardiff houses and distemper

Many Victorian and Edwardian houses in areas like Roath, Canton and Cathays still have distemper, an old chalky paint, on ceilings and sometimes walls, hidden under later coats. It is easy to spot when it fails: paint flakes off in sheets, and a powdery white residue comes off on your fingers.

Modern emulsion will not stick to distemper. It needs washing off where possible, or sealing with a stabilising primer made for powdery surfaces, before you repaint. In bad cases, a skim coat is the most reliable fix. Painting straight over it almost always ends with the new paint peeling within months.

What is the hardest colour to paint over?

Deep, saturated colours are the hardest to cover:

  • Dark reds and burgundy
  • Navy, dark blue and dark green
  • Black and charcoal
  • Bright yellow and orange, which show through as a warm tint under whites and pale neutrals

Going the other way is harder than people expect too: many light top coats, especially yellows and reds, have low opacity and need several coats to cover evenly.

To cover a dark colour efficiently, use a primer or basecoat first, either white or tinted grey depending on the new colour, then two top coats. That usually beats four or five coats of the finish colour on its own, both on cost and on the final result.

Stains, damp patches and nicotine

Water stains, smoke and nicotine, and marker pen will bleed straight through new emulsion, however many coats you apply. Fix the cause of any damp first, let the wall dry out, then seal the marks with a stain-blocking primer before painting. For a whole room affected by smoke, wash the walls and ceiling and seal everything before repainting. Dealing with black spots in corners or around windows? Frequently asked questions

Do I need to sand before painting over old paint?+

A light sand is usually worthwhile. It smooths out bits and old drips, and gives the new paint a better grip, especially on sheen finishes like silk, eggshell or gloss. Matt walls in good condition may only need cleaning and filling.

Can you paint emulsion over gloss?+

Not directly. Emulsion will not bond well to gloss. Sand the gloss to a dull finish, clean it, and use a suitable primer or undercoat first.

How many coats does it take to cover a dark colour?+

Usually three or more coats of a light colour, or a primer plus two top coats. A tinted primer or a mid-grey basecoat often saves a coat.

Should I paint over lining paper?+

Yes, lining paper is designed to be painted. It is a good way to get a smooth finish over sound but uneven walls. Allow it to dry fully after hanging and use a mist coat or diluted first coat if the paper is very absorbent.
