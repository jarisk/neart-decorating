Damp & mould

Black mould on walls: causes, cleaning and anti-mould paint

Updated 24 November 2026By Neart Decorating, Cardiff8 min read
Quick answer

Black mould on walls is almost always a sign of too much moisture, usually condensation on cold walls, sometimes a leak or penetrating damp. Fix the cause first: better ventilation and extraction, steadier heating and any repairs. Then clean the mould off with a fungicidal wash, let the wall dry fully and repaint with two coats of anti-mould paint. Anti-mould paint helps stop mould coming back on the surface, but on its own it won’t fix a damp problem.

What causes black mould on walls?

Mould needs moisture, and in most homes that moisture comes from everyday living. Cooking, showers, drying clothes indoors and even breathing all put water vapour into the air. When that warm, damp air meets a cold wall, it turns back into water. That is condensation, and walls that stay damp from it are where black mould starts.

That is why it tends to appear in the same places:

  • Corners of external walls, especially at ceiling height, where the wall is coldest
  • Around and below windows, where condensation runs off the glass
  • Behind wardrobes, beds and sofas pushed against outside walls, where air can’t circulate
  • Bathrooms and kitchens, especially ceilings above showers and walls near the cooker
  • Bedrooms, where people sleep with the door shut and the heating off

It is worst from autumn to spring, when walls are cold, windows stay shut and washing dries on radiators. In Cardiff, many Victorian and Edwardian terraces in areas such as Roath, Canton and Cathays have solid walls and cold bay windows, and these rooms can get mould even when people are careful.

Is it condensation or damp?

Before you clean or paint anything, work out where the water is coming from. Condensation is the most common cause, but a leak or water getting in from outside will bring mould straight back however good the paint is.

Wales gets a lot of wind-driven rain, so blocked gutters, cracked render and failed pointing are common causes of penetrating damp here. If a damp patch lines up with a problem outside, fix that first. Painting the inside won’t keep the rain out.

Fix the cause before you paint

This is the step most people skip, and it is why mould keeps coming back. For condensation, the aim is to produce less moisture, remove what you do produce and keep walls from getting too cold:

  • Use extractor fans while cooking and showering, and leave them running afterwards. Repairing a weak or broken fan often makes the biggest difference.
  • Keep trickle vents open on windows, and open a window for a few minutes after a shower or a cooking session.
  • Dry washing outside where you can, or in one room with a window open or a dehumidifier running.
  • Keep heating steady. A low, even temperature keeps walls warmer than short bursts of heat in a cold house.
  • Leave a gap behind furniture on outside walls so air can move.

If you rent, mould is something to report to your landlord. In Wales, landlords have a legal duty under the Renting Homes (Wales) Act to keep homes fit to live in, and damp and mould are on the list of things that count. Citizens Advice, Shelter Cymru or your local council can help if you need advice.

How to clean black mould off walls safely

Small patches of mould caused by condensation can usually be cleaned by a reasonably fit, healthy adult. Anyone with asthma, allergies, breathing problems or a weakened immune system should ask someone else to do it, or get professional help. The same goes for large areas, mould that keeps coming back quickly, or mould caused by sewage or flood water.

  1. Protect yourself. Wear rubber gloves, goggles and a mask suitable for dust, and open the windows. Keep children and pets out of the room.
  2. Don’t dry-brush it. Brushing or vacuuming dry mould spreads spores around the room.
  3. Use a fungicidal mould wash made for walls, mixed and applied exactly as the label says. Washing-up liquid and water will shift surface marks, but a fungicidal product does a better job of killing what is left in the surface.
  4. Wipe gently with a cloth, rinsing it often. Bag up the used cloths and throw them away.
  5. Leave the wall to dry completely, then check it. If the staining is still there, repeat the treatment.

Never mix cleaning products. Bleach mixed with other cleaners, such as ammonia or some acidic bathroom products, can give off dangerous fumes. Use one product at a time, follow the label, and keep the room well ventilated.

Mouldy wallpaper or silicone sealant is usually best replaced rather than cleaned, because mould grows behind it where you can’t treat it. If the plaster underneath has been damp for a long time and is crumbling or blown, it may need repairing before painting. See our plastering service.

Does anti-mould paint work?

Yes, as part of the fix, but not on its own. Anti-mould paints contain a fungicide (biocide) that stops mould growing on the paint surface. In a bathroom or kitchen that is ventilated properly, it can keep walls and ceilings clean for years. Manufacturers typically claim around five to ten years, depending on the product and the room.

What it won’t do is stop condensation forming or water getting into a wall. On a wall that stays wet, mould will eventually grow on the grime that collects on the paint, or the paint will blister and peel.

A few things to know before you buy:

  • It is usually the top coat. The fungicide only works on the surface, so most anti-mould paints shouldn’t be painted over with ordinary emulsion or covered with wallpaper. Check the tin.
  • Colour choice can be limited. Many are sold in white or a small range of colours, though some can be tinted.
  • Kitchen and bathroom paints vary. They are wipeable and cope with steam, and many contain a mould inhibitor. If mould is your main problem, choose one labelled anti-mould.
  • Older solid walls need care. In older Cardiff homes with lime plaster, a breathable paint system may be a better choice than a thick, sealing coating. Get advice before painting.

How to paint over black mould

  1. Deal with the cause and give the wall time to dry out properly.
  2. Clean the mould off with a fungicidal wash as above, and let it dry fully.
  3. Remove anything loose. Scrape off flaking paint, take off any mouldy wallpaper and fill cracks or holes. Sand smooth and wipe the dust away.
  4. Prime or stain-block if needed. Old mould can leave grey or brown staining that shows through emulsion. A stain-blocking primer that is compatible with your anti-mould paint stops it bleeding through. Bare plaster or filler needs priming too. See our mist coat guide for new plaster.
  5. Apply two coats of anti-mould paint, leaving the recoat time on the tin between them. Paint the whole wall or ceiling rather than just the patch, so the finish and protection are even.
  6. Let it cure before heavy steam. In a bathroom, keep showers short and the fan on for the first day or two, or follow the drying time on the tin.

Don’t paint over live mould with normal emulsion: it usually shows through within weeks. Frequently asked questions

Can I just paint over black mould?+

No. Paint over live mould and it will usually come back through the new coat, often within a few weeks. Fix the moisture problem, clean the mould off with a fungicidal wash, let the wall dry fully and then repaint with anti-mould paint.

Can I put normal emulsion over anti-mould paint?+

Usually not. Most anti-mould paints work only when they are the top coat, so covering them with ordinary emulsion or wallpaper removes the protection. Check the manufacturer’s instructions on the tin.

Why does black mould keep coming back on my bedroom wall?+

Because the wall is still getting damp, usually from condensation on a cold outside wall. Keep the heating steady, open trickle vents, leave a gap behind furniture and avoid drying washing in the room. If it still returns, check for a leak or penetrating damp from outside.

Is bleach good for cleaning black mould?+

Bleach can lighten mould stains, but a fungicidal mould wash made for walls is usually the better choice for painted surfaces. Whatever you use, follow the label, ventilate the room and never mix bleach with other cleaning products.

How long does anti-mould paint last?+

Manufacturers typically claim around five to ten years of protection, depending on the product. It lasts longest in rooms with good ventilation. If condensation or damp hasn’t been fixed, mould can return much sooner.
