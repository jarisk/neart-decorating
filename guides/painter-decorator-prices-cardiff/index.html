How much do painters and decorators charge in Cardiff?

Updated 21 November 2026By Neart Decorating, Cardiff9 min read
Quick answer

Most painters and decorators in Cardiff price by the job rather than the hour. As a guide for 2026, expect around £20–£40 an hour or £180–£325 a day for one decorator, and roughly £400–£600 to paint the walls, ceiling and woodwork of an average double bedroom. Our own guide prices start from £12 per m² for interior repaints and £25 per m² for exterior painting.

How much do painters and decorators charge per hour?

Across the UK, national price guides for 2026 put a painter and decorator at roughly £20–£40 an hour, and Cardiff sits comfortably in that range. London and the South East are higher; most of Wales is a little lower.

In practice, very few decorators charge by the hour for a whole room. An hourly or half-day rate is normally kept for small jobs: touching up after a repair, painting a single door, or a short list of snagging. For anything bigger you should expect a fixed price for the job, which is easier for you to budget and means you are not paying for the decorator’s tea breaks.

How much do you pay a painter per day?

For one experienced decorator outside London, day rates in 2026 typically fall between £180 and £325. A day usually means around seven to eight hours on site. A two-person team costs more per day but finishes in roughly half the time, so the total for the job often ends up similar.

Day rates make sense when the scope is hard to pin down, for example a run of small repairs across a house. For a defined job such as “three bedrooms, walls, ceilings and woodwork”, ask for a fixed written quote instead. You will know the full cost before anyone picks up a brush.

How much would a painter charge to paint a 12x12 room?

A 12 ft × 12 ft room is about 3.7 m × 3.7 m, a typical double bedroom. With a standard 2.4 m ceiling there are roughly 35 m² of wall to paint before you take off windows and doors, plus about 13 m² of ceiling.

For walls, ceiling and woodwork in two coats, expect to pay around £400–£600, and allow one to two days. If you only want the walls painted, the price drops noticeably, often into the £250–£400 range.

A 10 × 12 ft room (about 3 m × 3.7 m) is slightly smaller and usually costs £350–£550 for the same work.

A Cardiff note on ceiling height. Many Victorian and Edwardian terraces in areas like Roath, Canton, Cathays and Pontcanna have ceilings of 2.7–3 m. The same 12x12 floor then has 15–25% more wall to paint, and the ceiling is harder to reach, so the price is usually at the top of the range or a little above it.

How much is labour for painting a room?

Labour is most of what you pay for. For a typical bedroom, the paint itself usually costs £60–£150 using good trade emulsion for the walls and ceiling and a durable finish for the woodwork. The rest of the price covers the time it takes to do the job properly:

  • moving and covering furniture, and protecting floors
  • filling cracks and holes, sanding, and spot-priming repairs
  • cutting in neatly along ceilings, skirting and sockets
  • two full coats, with drying time between them
  • sanding and painting the woodwork
  • cleaning up and putting the room back at the end

Preparation is where cheap quotes usually cut corners. On older Cardiff houses, with years of layered paint and cracked plaster, it can be half the job. If walls need more than filling, a plaster repair or skim before decorating will give a far better finish than paint alone (and new plaster needs a mist coat before painting).

What affects the price of painting and decorating?

Condition of the walls

Smooth, sound walls are quick to paint. Cracks, nail holes, flaking paint and patched plaster all need filling and sanding first, and fresh plaster needs a mist coat before the top coats.

Colour change

Going from a dark or strong colour to a light one often needs an extra coat, sometimes a stain-blocking primer. Going lighter to lighter in a similar shade is the cheapest change.

Ceiling height and stairwells

High ceilings and stairwells take longer and need access equipment. They are often the most expensive part of a whole-house repaint.

Woodwork

Doors, frames, skirting, windows and banisters are slow, careful work. A room with four doors costs more than one with a single door, even if the floor area is the same.

Wallpaper

Stripping old paper, repairing the wall underneath and lining or hanging new paper all add time. This is the main difference between “painting” and “decorating” a room. See our wallpapering page if you want paper hung or a feature wall.

Empty or furnished

An empty room, such as a rental between tenants, is quicker to work in than a furnished family room that has to be moved and protected.

Exterior access

For outside work, scaffolding or access towers, the condition of the render and masonry, and the number of windows and fascias all affect the price. Exterior jobs are best booked for the drier months. Tired uPVC windows, doors and fascias can be colour-changed too: see uPVC spraying.

What is a fair price for painting a room?

A fair price is one that covers proper preparation, good paint and two full coats, and is set out clearly in writing. Rather than comparing totals alone, check that every quote includes:

  1. the rooms and surfaces included: walls, ceilings, woodwork, doors
  2. the preparation that will be done
  3. the number of coats
  4. the paint brand and finish, and whether paint is included
  5. protection of floors and furniture, and daily clean-up
  6. a start date and how long the work will take
  7. public liability insurance

Be cautious of a price well below the others with no written breakdown. It usually means one coat, little or no preparation, or extras added once the work has started.

Is it cheaper to paint a room yourself?

In pure cash terms, yes. If you already own brushes, rollers and dust sheets, painting a bedroom yourself costs little more than the paint, roughly £60–£150. If you are starting from scratch, add £30–£60 for tools and materials.

The real cost is time and finish. A first-timer usually needs a full weekend or more for a bedroom, and ceilings, stairwells, glossing woodwork and dark-to-light colour changes are where DIY results tend to show. Many homeowners do the simple rooms themselves and bring in a decorator for the hallway, stairs and landing or the main living spaces.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to paint a room?+

An average bedroom takes one to two days for walls, ceiling and woodwork with two coats. A small room with good walls can be done in a day; a room with a lot of filling, a dark colour to cover or a full set of doors and skirting usually takes two.

Is paint included in a decorator's price?+

It depends on the decorator, so always check. Some quote labour only and ask you to buy the paint; others supply trade paint and include it. A good written quote says which, and names the paint brand and finish.

Do decorators charge extra for ceilings?+

Ceilings are normally included when a whole room is being painted. On their own, or when they are very high, cracked or stained, they are usually priced separately because they take longer and need more preparation.

Is it cheaper to book a decorator in winter?+

Interior work is quieter in the winter months, so it is often easier to get the dates you want. Exterior work is best kept for the drier months from late spring to early autumn, which is when decorators get booked up.
