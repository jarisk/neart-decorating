Can you paint uPVC windows and doors?

Updated 21 November 2026By Neart Decorating, Cardiff7 min read
Quick answer

Yes, uPVC windows and doors can be painted, but not with ordinary paint. The frames need cleaning, keying and an adhesion primer, then a paint made for uPVC. Professionally sprayed, a window typically costs around £150–£200 and a door and frame £200–£250, a fraction of the price of replacing them.

How are uPVC windows and doors painted?

uPVC is a smooth, non-porous plastic, so ordinary paint has nothing to grip. A finish that lasts comes from the preparation and from using products made for the job:

  1. Mask everything. Glass, seals, hinges, handles, brickwork and paving are covered so only the frames are sprayed.
  2. Clean and degrease. Years of dirt, traffic film and silicone residue are removed with a specialist cleaner.
  3. Key the surface. A light abrasion gives the primer something to bond to.
  4. Prime. An adhesion primer is applied to bond to the plastic.
  5. Spray the colour. A paint made for uPVC goes on in thin, even coats, which gives a smooth factory-style finish with no brush marks.

Done this way, the colour bonds to the frame rather than sitting on top of it. This is exactly how we work on our uPVC spraying jobs around Cardiff.

Is it worth spraying uPVC rather than replacing it?

If your windows and doors still open, close and seal properly, and the problem is mostly how they look, spraying is usually far better value. New uPVC casement windows commonly cost several hundred pounds each fitted, while spraying a frame costs a fraction of that. You also avoid the mess of taking frames out and making good the reveals afterwards.

Replacement makes more sense if the frames are damaged or warped, the double glazing has failed (misting between the panes), the locks and hinges are worn out, or you want better energy efficiency.

Can you paint uPVC yourself?

It is possible with the right products: a uPVC cleaner, an adhesion primer and a paint made for uPVC, applied by brush, roller or aerosol. The results are rarely as good as spraying, though. Brushed paint shows marks on the smooth profiles, and getting a clean edge against glass and seals is slow work. The most common DIY problems are:

  • using ordinary gloss or masonry paint, which peels or cracks
  • skipping the cleaning and keying, so the paint never bonds
  • painting the rubber seals, which can stop windows closing properly
  • painting in damp or cold weather, so the finish never cures properly

For a single back door, DIY can be a reasonable choice. For windows on the front of the house, where everyone sees them every day, a professional spray finish is usually worth it.

How long does sprayed uPVC last?

With proper cleaning, keying, an adhesion primer and a paint made for uPVC, a sprayed finish stands up well to the Welsh weather and should look good for many years. As with any painted surface, most of the durability comes from the preparation. Afterwards, wash the frames occasionally with mild soapy water and avoid harsh solvents or abrasive pads.

The most popular uPVC colours

  • Anthracite grey (RAL 7016): by far the most popular. Modern and smart, and it suits almost any brick or render.
  • Black: crisp and striking, especially on white or light render.
  • Chartwell green: a soft grey-green that suits period and cottage-style homes.
  • Cream: warmer than white and good for traditional houses.
  • Agate grey and other light greys: a softer alternative to anthracite.

Most RAL colours can be matched, so you can also pick something to suit your house, or match new windows to an existing door.

Front door colours that work

A front door is the quickest way to change how a house looks from the street. Colours that consistently look good:

  • Navy and deep blue: classic, smart and welcoming, and they work with most brick and render.
  • Dark green: traditional and elegant, especially on period terraces.
  • Black or anthracite: timeless, and a safe choice if you plan to sell.
  • Muted sage, grey-blue or soft taupe: calmer and more modern.
  • A confident colour (deep red, mustard or teal): great for character, as long as it suits the rest of the street.

Look at the colour of your brick or render, roof and window frames first. A door that contrasts with the frames, such as navy with white frames or green with cream, usually looks more deliberate than one that is almost the same shade. Avoid very bright or neon colours if you are selling soon, and remember that dark colours on a sunny, south-facing door can get very hot.

Frequently asked questions

Can you paint uPVC with normal paint?+

No. Ordinary gloss or masonry paint is not designed to bond to plastic and tends to peel, crack or stay soft. uPVC needs thorough cleaning and keying, an adhesion primer and a paint made specifically for uPVC.

Can you spray woodgrain uPVC a solid colour?+

Yes. Woodgrain-effect uPVC can be cleaned, keyed and primed, then sprayed a solid colour such as anthracite grey, black or cream. A light texture from the grain may still show through, which most people never notice.

Can you spray composite doors?+

Yes, composite front doors can usually be sprayed in the same way as uPVC, with the right preparation and primer for the door's surface.

Does uPVC spraying affect the window warranty?+

It can. Some window manufacturers' guarantees exclude painted frames, so if your windows are still under a manufacturer's warranty, check the terms before you go ahead.

Can you spray uPVC in winter?+

Spraying needs dry weather and temperatures within the paint maker's limits, so outdoor work is weather-dependent. Spring to early autumn is the most reliable time, but dry, mild spells in winter can work too.
